Horizons
Observable sphere i
Observable universe — the sphere within which light has had time to reach us since the Big Bang (~46.5 Gly radius today). We cannot see or know anything beyond this. It grows slowly as time passes.
Reachable sphere i
Reachable universe — the smaller sphere of galaxies we could ever physically reach, even travelling at the speed of light forever. Dark energy is accelerating expansion, so galaxies beyond ~16.5 Gly are receding faster than light can ever close the gap. This sphere shrinks as time passes. This is the true measure of cosmic loneliness.
Hubble flow vectors i
Hubble flow — arrows showing the recession of space in every direction. Every galaxy beyond our Local Group moves away from us at v = H₀ × d. This is not motion through space — the space between galaxies is literally stretching. Arrows brighten as recession velocity increases with time.
Dark energy field i
Dark energy (Λ) — an unknown energy permeating all of space, making up ~69% of the universe's total energy content. It acts as a repulsive force: the more space exists between two objects, the stronger the push. This is why expansion accelerates. It was unknown until 1998. We have no idea what it is. Toggle on and advance time to see it dominate.
Force Fields
Hubble expansion field i
Hubble expansion field — blue particles stream outward from the Milky Way (scene origin). There is no centre of the universe — any galaxy would see exactly this same radial flow from its own position. Our simulation shows it from our position: Earth. Speed scales with distance (v = H(t)·d) — particles far from us move faster than those nearby. Particles beyond ~14 Gly move faster than light (Hubble radius). Switch to first-person to stand inside the field.
Gravitational binding field i
Gravitational binding — red-orange particles stream toward the nearest massive attractor (Local Group, Virgo, Shapley). Falls off as 1/r². Near the centre it overwhelms Hubble expansion — this is why bound structures exist. Further out it weakens until expansion wins completely.
Net force field i
Net resultant field — green particles show Hubble minus gravity at every point. Where particles flow outward: expansion wins, the region is unbound. Where they flow inward: gravity wins, the region is bound. The zero-velocity surface — where particles barely move — is the true boundary between the Local Group and the expanding universe. Watch it shrink toward us as dark energy strengthens with time.
Objects
Local Group labels i
Local Group — our gravitationally bound neighbourhood: the Milky Way, Andromeda (M31), the Magellanic Clouds, and ~80 smaller galaxies. Gravity overcomes expansion at this scale — these objects will not recede from each other. Andromeda will actually collide with the Milky Way in ~4.5 Gyr. Stars like Sirius and Alpha Centauri are bound to the Milky Way and do not expand away.
Galaxy clusters i
Galaxy clusters — the largest gravitationally bound structures: Virgo, Coma, Perseus, and the Shapley Attractor. These ARE receding from us via Hubble flow — they sit in the unbound universe beyond our Local Group. Watch them fly outward as you advance time.
Field galaxies i
Field galaxies — the ~2 trillion galaxies scattered throughout the observable universe. Colour encodes cosmological redshift: blue = nearby and slow, red = distant and fast-receding. All are expanding away from us. Click any one to inspect its recession velocity.
Photon paths i
Photon paths — click any galaxy or cluster, then fire a photon toward it. The photon travels at c but space between here and there is expanding. If the target is beyond the reachable horizon, the photon will never arrive — watch it slow and fade against the expansion. If within the reachable sphere, it will cross the distance eventually.
