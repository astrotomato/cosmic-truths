## Level 4 — Orion Arm & Local Bubble

**Scale**: 1 unit ≈ 20 light-years  
**Camera default**: 30 units out, 60° elevation

### The Local Bubble

```
The Local Bubble is a cavity in the interstellar medium (ISM)
approximately 300 ly in diameter, centered near the Sun.

Origin: A chain of ~15 supernovae over the last ~10–14 Myr
        (Zucker et al. 2022; Breitschwerdt et al. 2016)
Interior: Hot, low-density plasma (~10⁶ K, n ~ 0.005 cm⁻³)
Boundary: Dense shell of neutral hydrogen and molecular clouds

Five nearby star-forming regions sit on the Bubble's rim:
  Ophiuchus, Lupus, Chameleon, Corona Australis, Musca
  (All at ~450–520 ly — right on the shell)
```

```
Location:    2.2 – 3.2 AU
Population:  ~1.1–1.9 million objects > 1km diameter
             ~10⁹ objects > 100m

Notable objects:
  Ceres (dwarf planet):  R ≈ 473 km; a = 2.769 AU
  Vesta:                 R ≈ 265 km; a = 2.362 AU
```

### Lagrange Points — Earth-Moon System

| Point | Position | Distance from Earth | Stability | Notable use |
|-------|----------|--------------------|-----------| ------------|
| L1 | Between Earth and Moon | ~323,000 km | Unstable | Station-keeping; proposed Gateway station |
| L2 | Beyond Moon | ~445,000 km | Unstable | Far side lunar relay; proposed LUMIO mission |
| L3 | Opposite Moon | ~384,000 km (far side) | Unstable | No practical use; "Counter-Moon" of fiction |
| L4 | 60° ahead of Moon | 384,400 km | **Stable** | Kordylewski dust clouds; proposed colony site (L5 Society) |
| L5 | 60° behind Moon | 384,400 km | **Stable** | Same as L4; original L5 Society proposal (1974) |

**The L5 Society** was founded by Gerard O'Neill advocates in 1975 with the goal of building an O'Neill cylinder space colony at Earth-Moon L5. It merged with the National Space Institute in 1987 to form the National Space Society.

**Kordylewski clouds**: Kazimierz Kordylewski observed dust at L4/L5 in 1961. Confirmed photographically by Slíz-Balogh et al. (2018), Nature Astronomy 3, 30.

## Level 7 — Pale Blue Dot

The Pale Blue Dot photograph was taken on February 14, 1990 by the Voyager 1 spacecraft at a distance of 6.06 × 10⁹ km (40.5 AU) from Earth.

```
Voyager 1 distance at photo: 40.50 AU = 6.056 × 10⁹ km
Earth's angular diameter:    ~0.12 arcseconds = 5.8 × 10⁻⁷ radians
Earth occupies:              ~0.12 pixels in the original image
Photograph date:             February 14, 1990, 00:00 UTC
Camera:                      Voyager 1 narrow-angle camera
Filter:                      Blue (440 nm), Green (530 nm), Violet (410 nm)
Suggested by:                Carl Sagan (as a "last look homeward")
```

Carl Sagan's meditation on the photograph, delivered at Cornell University on October 13, 1994, is considered among the most significant pieces of scientific humanism in the 20th century. It appears in full in *Pale Blue Dot: A Vision of the Human Future in Space* (1994, Random House).
